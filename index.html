Chandan Bhat

University of Cambridge

I work on inverse problems: how to recover an image or a physical structure from measurements that are sparse, noisy, or incomplete.

At Cambridge, I develop Bayesian reconstruction methods for optical interferometry. My current focus is on reconstructing how an object’s appearance changes with wavelength, and understanding which features the measurements actually constrain.

Before this, I worked on electromagnetic field prediction, radar imaging, and sensor selection. I completed my M.S. and Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering at IIT Madras in 2023.

Research

Optical-interferometric imaging

Optical interferometers provide information about a source at a limited set of spatial frequencies. I develop polychromatic models that infer spatial structure and spectral variation together, using physical forward models, Bayesian priors, and variational inference.

Current work at Cambridge. Python, JAX, and NIFTy.

Sampling and electromagnetic inverse problems

My doctoral work studied field prediction and inverse imaging from incomplete electromagnetic measurements. One part of this work asks which sensor positions and frequencies are most useful for subsurface radar imaging.

Radar-imaging implementation · Total-field imaging paper

Machine learning for physical models

I co-developed a tandem neural-network method for multiband antenna design, coupling an inverse network to a CNN surrogate of electromagnetic simulations. I am also developing LLM-assisted workflows to inspect reconstruction diagnostics and support the choice of subsequent experiments.

The LLM-assisted reconstruction work is ongoing.

Research software

I maintain MATLAB implementations of several of my research methods, with examples and numerical checks.

  • Subsurface radar sensor optimisation

    Selection of sensor positions and frequencies using a scattering operator and signal-subspace methods.

  • Total-field inverse imaging

    Research code accompanying work on inverse imaging using total-field measurements.

  • Compressive-sensing field prediction

    Code for predicting scattered electromagnetic fields from a limited set of measurements.

  • OAM communication with circular arrays

    Simulation of array fields, mode purity, and channel capacity for OAM radio links.
